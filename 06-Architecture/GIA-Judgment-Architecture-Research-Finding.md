# GIA Judgment Architecture — Research Finding

**Research item:** #74

**Status:** Research complete / closed

**Current confidence:** Strong, provisional, falsifiable

**Purpose:** Preserve the research finding from #74 before applying the architecture to real GIA work. This is a research record, not a formal GIA standard and not GIA Judgment Framework v1.0.

---

## 1. Research Question

Can GIA judgment be represented by a minimal reusable set of core decision-impact dimensions without requiring a fourth dimension, numerical risk scoring, or a large decision tree?

The research ultimately focused on whether three dimensions were sufficient:

1. **Treatment**
2. **Authority**
3. **Consequence / Reversibility**

---

## 2. Research Finding

The #74 research program supports the provisional conclusion that:

> **Treatment + Authority + Consequence/Reversibility form the minimal reusable core of GIA treatment judgment.**

This does **not** mean these are the only factors GIA may need to consider. The research repeatedly found factors that materially affect decisions without demonstrating that they are independent decision-impact dimensions.

The important distinction is:

> **A factor can be important to GIA judgment without being an independent judgment dimension.**

---

## 3. Current Architecture

The research suggests a layered architecture rather than a flat three-factor checklist:

### Basis

Before judging a treatment, GIA must have a sufficient basis for judgment.

This includes relevant facts, intent, known unknowns, dependencies, and other information needed to understand what is actually being decided.

If the basis is insufficient, GIA should not manufacture certainty. Appropriate responses may include clarification, verification, bounded treatment, asking, or escalation.

### Core Admissibility

Once there is sufficient basis, evaluate the proposed treatment through the three core dimensions:

#### 1. Treatment

**What exactly is GIA being asked to do?**

The treatment is the actual action or set of actions being authorized, evaluated, or selected. Scope and scale are properties of the treatment rather than automatically separate dimensions.

#### 2. Authority

**Is GIA legitimately authorized to perform the treatment?**

Authority includes the relevant source of permission and the resolution of authority conflicts. Conflicting legitimate authorities do not automatically create another dimension; if the hierarchy cannot be resolved, the decision reaches a boundary.

#### 3. Consequence / Reversibility

**What happens if GIA performs the treatment, and how recoverable is the result?**

This captures material impact, reversibility, recoverability, and the consequences of acting or waiting where those consequences affect the decision.

### Selection

If multiple treatments are admissible, the problem changes from:

> **Is this treatment admissible?**

to:

> **Which admissible treatment should be selected?**

Selection may use a legitimate objective, stated preference, established organizational default, or other valid selection criterion.

If no legitimate basis exists for choosing among otherwise admissible treatments, GIA should not invent a preference.

### Boundary / Resolution

When GIA cannot legitimately resolve the decision, the correct behavior is to stop rather than manufacture an answer.

Possible boundary behaviors include:

- Clarify
- Verify
- Ask
- Escalate
- Defer pending required information or authority resolution

---

## 4. Major Research Tests

The framework was deliberately attacked from multiple directions, including:

- Individual boundary testing
- Combined/adversarial cases
- Timing and urgency
- Dependency
- Scale and accumulation
- Known unknowns / incomplete information
- Competing acceptable treatments
- Preference-free decisions
- Adversarial objectives
- Authority collisions
- Blind taxonomy testing
- Minimality testing
- Sufficiency testing
- Composability
- Boundary behavior
- Operational usefulness
- Falsifiability / closure criteria

A recurring result was that apparent candidates for a fourth dimension either:

1. mapped into Treatment, Authority, or Consequence/Reversibility; or
2. belonged to a supporting layer such as Basis, Selection, Context, or Resolution.

---

## 5. Why the Three Dimensions Are Considered Minimal

Each core dimension performs decision work that the other two cannot replace.

### Without Treatment

GIA cannot distinguish materially different actions when authority and consequences are otherwise comparable.

### Without Authority

GIA cannot distinguish an authorized action from an unauthorized action when the treatment and consequences are otherwise comparable.

### Without Consequence / Reversibility

GIA cannot distinguish materially different impacts of otherwise comparable treatments performed under the same authority.

Therefore, removing any one of the three causes previously distinguishable judgments to collapse incorrectly.

---

## 6. Why Apparent Fourth Dimensions Did Not Become Dimensions

The research repeatedly encountered factors that looked like possible fourth dimensions.

Examples included:

- **Timing / urgency** — can change the treatment or its consequences, including the consequences of waiting.
- **Dependencies** — can change the effective treatment or its consequences.
- **Scale** — is part of the actual treatment being evaluated; a large migration is one treatment with many targets, not merely many unrelated low-risk actions.
- **Uncertainty / factual validity** — affects the basis for judgment rather than independently defining treatment admissibility.
- **Objective / preference** — belongs primarily to selection among admissible treatments.
- **Authority conflict** — is a problem of authority resolution, not a new decision-impact dimension.

The standard for adding another dimension is therefore deliberately demanding.

> **A proposed Dimension #4 must demonstrate independent decision impact.**

It should not be promoted merely because GIA needs to consider it.

---

## 7. Closure Standard

#74 was closed after the proposed proof/closure criteria were satisfied:

1. **Coverage** — independently discovered decision categories map to the architecture.
2. **Minimality** — removing any core dimension causes meaningful classification failures.
3. **Sufficiency** — no independent fourth core category emerged from the test program.
4. **Composability** — the dimensions continue to work when multiple difficult factors occur together.
5. **Boundary integrity** — uncertainty, unresolved preference, and authority conflicts produce principled stops rather than invented decisions.
6. **Domain independence** — the architecture is not limited to one type of GIA work.
7. **Operational usefulness** — the model supports categorical judgment without numerical scoring or an enormous decision tree.
8. **Falsifiability** — a clear condition exists for reopening the research.

The blind taxonomy test was especially important because it first identified decision categories independently and only afterward compared them with the proposed architecture.

---

## 8. Falsification / Reopening Condition

#74 should be reopened if real GIA work produces a case where:

> **The correct judgment materially changes while Treatment, Authority, and Consequence/Reversibility remain materially equivalent, and the difference cannot be explained by Basis, Selection, Context, or Resolution.**

That would constitute a legitimate candidate for an independent fourth decision-impact dimension.

A factor does **not** become Dimension #4 merely because it is important, recurring, inconvenient, or worthy of consideration.

It must demonstrate independent decision impact.

---

## 9. Current Status and Limits

### Established by research

The three-dimensional core has earned provisional status as the current minimal reusable core of GIA judgment.

### Not yet established

The architecture has not yet been validated across a sufficiently broad body of real GIA decisions to justify calling it a formal v1.0 standard.

### Therefore

**Do not formalize this document as GIA Judgment Framework v1.0 yet.**

This document records the research finding and establishes the starting architecture for operational validation.

---

## 10. Next Phase

The research phase is complete. The next phase is real-world validation:

> **Document → Apply → Observe → Validate → Then build**

### Apply

Use the architecture against actual GIA decisions rather than deliberately constructed research cases.

### Observe

Record cases that fit, partially fit, or appear not to fit.

### Validate

Look for recurring patterns and genuine independent anomalies rather than isolated oddities.

### Then build

If the architecture survives real-world validation, evaluate whether it has earned formalization as **GIA Judgment Framework v1.0**.

If a genuine independent fourth dimension emerges, reopen #74.

---

## 11. Core Principle Preserved by #74

> **Build the foundation. Prove the workflow. Let the next layer earn its way into existence.**

The practical lesson from #74 is:

> **We did not prove that the framework can never change. We established that the framework has earned the right to be tested in the real world.**
